🎓 Language School · Friday Edition · Slang & Fun
🇩🇪 Su learns German
FEIERABEND
/ FYE-er-AH-bend /
Quitting time · the sacred end of the workday
DE: "Es ist endlich Feierabend — Zeit zum Feiern!"
EN: "It's finally quitting time — time to celebrate!"
🧠 Memory trick: "Feier" = party + "Abend" = evening. Germans literally invented a word that means the party starts when work ends. Basically, they scheduled joy into the language.
✨ Bonus: "Ich mache Feierabend" = "I'm calling it a day / clocking out"
🇧🇷 Alex learns Brazilian Portuguese
ROLAR
/ ho-LAH /
To happen · to go down · to be on (versatile slang)
PT: "Vai rolar uma festa hoje à noite?"
EN: "Is there a party happening tonight?"
🧠 Memory trick: It literally means "to roll" — as in, the night is rolling, things are rolling. When Brazilians say "vai rolar" they mean it's on, it's happening, the energy is building.
✨ Bonus: "Não vai rolar" = "It's not happening / it's a no-go" — the most polite way to cancel plans in São Paulo.
